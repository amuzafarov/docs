Synclify is built around a simple security principle: access only what is needed, store only what is necessary, and protect everything in between. Every connection you make — to Google Sheets, Airtable, Notion, or Webflow — uses industry-standard OAuth authentication, your data in transit is protected by TLS 1.3, and your source data is never retained in Synclify's systems after a sync completes.

## Authentication & credentials

All source connections in Synclify — Google Sheets, Airtable, Notion, and Webflow — authenticate exclusively through OAuth. You authorize Synclify via each service's own login flow; Synclify never asks for, receives, or stores your passwords.

Once you complete an OAuth authorization, Synclify stores the resulting access token so it can authenticate future sync requests on your behalf. Every token is:

- **Encrypted at rest using AES-256-GCM** — a symmetric encryption standard used across the financial and healthcare industries
- **Stored per-connection** — each source or Webflow site you connect has its own isolated token
- **Used only for sync requests** — tokens are never shared between connections or used for any purpose other than performing the sync you configured

## Data in transit

Every request Synclify makes — reading from your sources and writing to Webflow — travels over **TLS 1.3**, the latest version of the Transport Layer Security protocol. TLS 1.3 provides stronger encryption and faster handshakes than its predecessors, and it eliminates legacy cipher suites that are vulnerable to downgrade attacks.

This applies to all traffic: reading from Google Sheets, Airtable, or Notion; writing to your Webflow CMS Collections; and any communication between your browser and the Synclify dashboard.

## Your source data

Synclify reads your source data — rows from Google Sheets, records from Airtable, entries from Notion — **only at the moment a sync runs**. That data is immediately written to your Webflow CMS Collections and is not retained, cached, or logged in Synclify's systems afterward.

Your spreadsheets and databases remain entirely your own. Synclify does not copy them, index them, or use them for any purpose beyond fulfilling the sync you configured.

## Access scopes

When you connect each service, Synclify requests the minimum OAuth scopes required to perform syncs:

| Service | Access requested |
|---|---|
| **Google Sheets** | Read-only access to the spreadsheets you select |
| **Airtable** | Read-only access to the bases you authorize |
| **Notion** | Read-only access to the databases you share |
| **Webflow** | Write access to CMS Collections (required to create and update items) |

<Note>
  Synclify requests the minimum permissions needed to perform syncs. It never writes to Google Sheets, Airtable, or Notion — those connections are strictly read-only.
</Note>

## Revoking access

You can disconnect any source or Webflow site at any time from the **Connections** section of your Synclify settings. Disconnecting a source immediately revokes Synclify's OAuth token for that service — Synclify can no longer read from it or write to it until you reconnect.

<Tip>
  You can also revoke Synclify's access directly from the connected apps settings in Google, Airtable, Notion, or Webflow. This is useful if you want to confirm revocation at the source level, independent of Synclify's dashboard.
</Tip>

## Client portal security

When you invite a client to their site's portal, that login is scoped to a **single Webflow site**. Client portal users:

- Cannot view or access any other client's site or data
- Cannot see your agency credentials or account settings
- Cannot connect new sources or modify sync configurations — they can trigger manual syncs and view sync logs only

This means you can give clients visibility into their own syncs without exposing your agency dashboard or other clients' workspaces.
