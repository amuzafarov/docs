---
title: "Archive and delete"
description: "Retire a connection without losing its history, or remove it permanently."
icon: "box-archive"
---

Two ways to retire a connection. One is reversible in spirit; the other is not reversible at all.

## Archive

Archived connections stop syncing and move out of your main list into **Archive** in the sidebar. Their history stays.

Use archive when a campaign ended, a collection is dormant, or you want the connection out of the way without destroying the record of what it did.

## Delete

Deleting stops all sync jobs and removes the connection permanently.

<Warning>
**This cannot be undone.** Synclify makes you prove you mean it: you must type the connection's **exact name** *and* give a written reason. The delete button stays disabled until both are filled.
</Warning>

Deleting a connection:

- stops all its sync jobs immediately
- removes the connection and its configuration
- **does not** delete the CMS items it created in Webflow — those stay in your site
- **does not** touch your Airtable data

## When you must delete

Two hard requirements in the product force a delete:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Reusing a Webflow collection">
    A Webflow collection can back only one live connection. To point a different source at a collection that's already connected, delete the existing connection first.

    *"Webflow collection already connected — Delete the existing connection for this Webflow collection before creating another one."*
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Disconnecting a data source">
    An account can't be disconnected while any non-archived, non-deleted connection depends on it. The dialog becomes **Delete connections first** and lists up to five blockers.

    *"Delete active connections before disconnecting this data source."*
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

In both cases archiving is enough — archived connections don't block.

## Before you delete

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check what depends on it">
    Is this the only thing keeping a collection populated? Deleting the connection leaves the CMS items in place, but nothing will update them again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consider archiving instead">
    Archive stops syncing and preserves history. If you might want the audit trail — or the configuration — archive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Note the configuration">
    Mapping, direction, and per-field ownership go with the connection. Rebuilding a large mapping by hand is tedious.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Deleting

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the connection">
    Go to the connection's detail page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Delete connection">
    Read the warning: *"Stops all sync jobs. This cannot be undone."*
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type the exact name">
    Character for character. This is the guard against deleting the wrong one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give a reason">
    Free text, required. It's recorded with the deletion.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    The connection and its jobs are gone.
  </Step>
</Steps>
